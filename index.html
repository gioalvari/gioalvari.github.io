What I've built

Currently at Enel, one of the largest utilities in the world. Deliberately described without internal system names.

  1. 2024 — now · Agentic AI · in production · Italy & Spain

    Agents that do real operational work

    I built, from scratch, an AI-based incident resolution programme for IT operations, running today in two countries. The agents read the same procedures a human engineer would follow, query the monitoring tools and network devices those procedures point to, reason over what comes back, decide whether to keep going — and write their conclusions into the ticketing platform where the work actually lives.

    One deployment covers a control room handling roughly a thousand incidents and eight thousand device diagnostics a month. I owned architecture and development decisions end-to-end, and ran it with operations teams in Rome and Seville, in Italian, Spanish and English.

    The hard part was never the orchestration. It was earning the trust of engineers who are accountable when something breaks — which is why nothing reaches production without their sign-off.

    LangChain · LlamaIndex · AWS Bedrock · FastAPI · Kubernetes · pgvector

  2. 2024 — now · Incident automation at group scale

    Ninety percent of a multinational's IT incidents

    I took ownership of the group's incident-automation platform and, by designing and shipping new capability into it, grew its coverage to around 90% of the company's IT incidents — with roughly 5% now closing end-to-end with no human involved at all.

  3. 2023 — now · Forecasting · zero to production · team of 5

    Forecasting that executives plan against

    A multi-year programme I led from nothing to production, under attention all the way up to CEO level: hierarchical forecasting of customer churn and payment-default disconnections as a function of price evolution — at aggregate level and down to micro-clusters — with conformal prediction for calibrated uncertainty and scenario simulation of how the customer base evolves.

    I owned architecture and delivery, managed a team of five, and ran the relationship with the business unit and executive stakeholders.

    The point wasn't a better number. It was giving planners a calibrated interval instead of a single confident-looking figure — which changed how the decisions were made.

    statsmodels · Nixtla · skforecast · conformal prediction · Spark · FastAPI

  4. 2025 · RAG · in production · serverless

    A document assistant you can trust with audit material

    Internal audit documentation is exactly the kind of corpus where a helpful, over-confident chatbot is worse than no chatbot. This one answers only from documents the person asking is actually cleared to see, and cites its sources. Serverless on AWS, shipped through a governed pipeline with security gating.

  5. 2020 — 2023 · ML at scale

    Machine learning on eighteen million contracts

    Before the LLM work: churn and claim prediction across 18M customer contracts on Spark, cutting churn by 15% and saving €2.2M annually; customer segmentation and cross-selling models for electrification; and real-time anomaly detection for battery energy storage systems, deployable on edge.

    PySpark · XGBoost · CatBoost · H2O · MLflow · Airflow

Things I build on my own time

Mostly to understand what happens underneath the abstractions I use at work. Exploratory, not production software — that distinction matters to me.

  • RadixForge C++17

    When ten agents share the same two-thousand-token system prompt, why compute it ten times? A radix-tree KV-cache orchestrator that shares common prompt prefixes across agents, built on native llama.cpp APIs.

  • Agent Memory Layer C++17

    A transparent OpenAI-compatible proxy that gives any agent persistent, searchable memory across sessions. Local embeddings, SQLite vector storage, temporal-decay retrieval. Includes 37 unit and integration tests, with batch embeddings benchmarked at up to 11× throughput on Apple Silicon.

  • global-forecasting Python

    Train a single model across many time series, with automatic lag generation, time-series cross-validation and zero-shot forecasting.

  • hybridforecast Python

    A library for testing hybrid forecasting models quickly: decompose a series, forecast each component separately, recombine. Most of the work in hybrid forecasting is plumbing — this removes it so you can compare approaches instead.

  • ats-checker Python

    Scores a CV against a job description using local LLM embeddings and TF-IDF, then suggests concrete edits. Built because I got tired of guessing what the filter wanted.

Older work — bioinformatics, Kaggle competitions, early forecasting experiments — lives on my first account, github.com/Dhonveli.

How I got here

I started in biology, not software. My master's was in computational biology at the University of Trento, and I spent a year at DKFZ and EMBL in Heidelberg working on single-cell eQTL mapping — trying to find which genetic variants change gene expression, in data where almost every signal is noise wearing a convincing costume. That work became a paper in Genome Biology; the containerised pipeline is still on my old GitHub account.

I don't think of that as a previous life. It's where the part of my work I value most comes from. In biology you learn very quickly that a result which looks good is not the same as a result which is true, and that the difference is entirely a matter of how carefully you set up the test. That instinct turns out to be the scarcest thing in applied AI right now — everyone can get a demo working; far fewer can tell you whether it actually works.

Then I moved into industry. Six years at Enel: first machine learning at scale on customer data, then a forecasting programme I grew from an idea into a production system with a team behind it, and now agentic systems doing operational work in two countries. The through-line is that I've always preferred problems where being wrong has consequences.

I'm drawn to energy, pharma and bio, and to organisations doing something worth doing. I live in Florence and work in Italian, English and Spanish.
